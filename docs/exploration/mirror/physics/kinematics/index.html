Chapter 1 · Kinematics

the description of motion

Kinematics describes motion with three quantities: where something is, how fast it is moving, and how that is changing. It doesn’t ask why.

Everything in this chapter comes from one ball, thrown straight up at 9.80 m/s. Once it leaves the hand, the only thing acting on it is gravity, so it is in free fall. That is true going up as well as coming down.

It rises for 1.00 s, stops 4.90 m up, and lands 2.00 s after launch. The figure runs at half speed.

1.1 · Position

displacement and distance

Position y is where the ball is, measured from an origin. Here the origin is the launch point, and up is positive.

Displacement Δy = yf − y0 is the net change in position. It is a vector: it has a size and a direction (the black arrow beside the axis).

Distance is the total length of the path. It is a scalar and never negative.

displacementdistance
launch → top+4.90 m4.90 m

1.2 · Velocity

and speed, which is not the same

Velocity v=ΔyΔtv = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta t} is how fast position changes, with a direction. Speed is its size, with no direction.

The arrow is the velocity. It starts at +9.80 m/s, shrinks to zero at the top, then grows pointing down until −9.80 m/s at landing. The speeds at launch and landing are the same; the velocities are not.

The same split applies to averages over the whole flight:

average velocityΔy/Δt=0/2.00=0 m/s\Delta y/\Delta t = 0 / 2.00 = 0\ \text{m/s}
average speeddistance/Δt=9.80/2.00=4.90 m/s\text{distance}/\Delta t = 9.80 / 2.00 = 4.90\ \text{m/s}

1.3 · Acceleration

the rate the velocity changes

Acceleration a=ΔvΔta = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}. For this ball it is −9.80 m/s² the whole time: going up, at the top, and coming down. The red arrow never changes.

The faint balls are a strobe photograph, one every 0.2 s. Going up, the gaps shrink by gτ² = 0.39 m each time. Coming down, the images land on exactly the same heights.

Speeding up or slowing down? Compare signs. When v and a have opposite signs the ball slows (going up). When they have the same sign it speeds up (coming down). Both are acceleration. There is no separate “deceleration”.

1.4 · Motion graphs

slopes and areas

The three graphs plot the same flight. The position graph is drawn to scale: its heights are the ball’s real heights.

  • slope of x–t = velocity
  • slope of v–t = acceleration
  • area under v–t = displacement
  • area under a–t = change in velocity

Constant acceleration gives a flat a–t line, a straight v–t line and a parabola for x–t. Each graph is one “power” lower than the one above it.

1.5 · The kinematic equations

for constant acceleration only

Five quantities describe this motion: Δy, v0, v, a, t. Each equation leaves one out, so pick the one without the quantity you don’t know and don’t need.

v=v0+atv = v_0 + atΔy
Δy=v0t+12at2\Delta y = v_0 t + \tfrac12 at^2v
v2=v02+2aΔyv^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta yt
Δy=12(v0+v)t\Delta y = \tfrac12(v_0 + v)ta
Δy=vt−12at2\Delta y = vt - \tfrac12 at^2v0

How high does it go? At the top v = 0, and t isn’t needed: 0 = 9.80² + 2(−9.80)Δy, so Δy = 4.90 m.

1.6 · Projectile motion

two motions at once

A second ball is thrown at the same time with the same upward 9.80 m/s, plus 6.00 m/s sideways.

It stays level with the first ball the whole way and lands at the same moment. The vertical motion is free fall, and the sideways speed doesn’t change it. Its shadow on the floor moves in equal 1.20 m steps: the horizontal motion is uniform.

1.7 · Reference frames

velocity depends on who is looking

Every velocity is measured relative to something. Rain falls 7.0 m/s straight down relative to the road; from a car doing 10.0 m/s it slants at 12.21 m/s.

Write vAB for “A relative to B” (R rain, C car, G ground). Velocities chain, inner letters matching:

vAC=vAB+vBCv_\text{AC} = v_\text{AB} + v_\text{BC}

So vRC = vRG + vGC, added tip to tail. Swapping letters flips the arrow: from the car the road moves backward, vGC = −vCG. The two parts are at right angles, so ∣vRC∣=10.02+7.02=12.21 m/s|v_\text{RC}| = \sqrt{10.0^2 + 7.0^2} = 12.21\ \text{m/s}, at tan⁡θ=10.07.0\tan\theta = \frac{10.0}{7.0}, θ = 55.0° from vertical. 1.8 · Crossing a river

two velocities, added tip to tail

A boat’s velocity relative to the shore is its velocity through the water plus the water’s velocity relative to the shore:

vBS=vBW+vWSv_\text{BS} = v_\text{BW} + v_\text{WS}

Both boats do 2.5 m/s through water flowing at 1.5 m/s, across 24 m.

1.9 · AP Physics C · Calculus kinematics

when the acceleration changes

The equations of 1.5 need constant a. This car’s a = 6.0 − 1.5t m/s² fades to zero at t1 = 4.00 s, so integrate instead. It starts from rest at the line: v0 = 0, x0 = 0.

1.10 · AP Physics C · Motion from x(t)

differentiate down, integrate up

Given x(t), differentiate. For x = t³ − 3t:

v=3t2−3a=6tv = 3t^2 - 3 \qquad a = 6t

It starts left at 3.00 m/s and turns at t = 1.00 s, x = −2.00 m. Over 2 s: displacement 2.00 m, distance 6.00 m.

Going back up needs initial conditions: from a = 6t, v = 3t² + C1 and x = t³ + C1t + C2; v(0) = −3 and x(0) = 0 give C1 = −3, C2 = 0.

Every 0.2 s; the trip back is drawn in front.
