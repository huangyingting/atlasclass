Chapter 3 · Composite, implicit and inverse functions

the chain rule and its kin

Most functions are built by feeding one function into another. Their derivatives come from one rule: rates of change multiply along the chain.

Turn the big gear once and the small gear on the middle shaft turns 3 times; that shaft turns the last gear 2 times for each of its turns. So the last gear turns 3 × 2 = 6 times as fast as the first.

That is the chain rule. The rest of the chapter uses it to differentiate curves that aren’t functions at all (implicit differentiation), to find the slope of an inverse without a formula for it, and to take derivatives of derivatives.

3.1 · The chain rule

rates multiply

If y depends on u and u depends on x, then y changes with x at the product of the two rates.

Count teeth. The x gear’s 30 teeth push 30 teeth of the 10-tooth pinion, so du/dx = 30/10 = 3. The u shaft’s 20-tooth gear drives the 10-tooth y gear: dy/du = 2.

dydx=dydu⋅dudx=2⋅3=6\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\cdot\frac{du}{dx} = 2\cdot 3 = 6

In function notation, for y = f(g(x)):

ddx f(g(x))=f′(g(x))⋅g′(x)\frac{d}{dx}\,f\big(g(x)\big) = f'\big(g(x)\big)\cdot g'(x)

The derivative of the outside, evaluated at the inside, times the derivative of the inside. For example ddx(x2+1)5=5(x2+1)4⋅2x\frac{d}{dx}(x^2+1)^{5} = 5(x^2+1)^4\cdot 2x.

Gears turn at constant rates; most chains don’t. The rule still holds at each instant, with each rate taken where the chain is at that moment.

3.2 · The chain rule on a graph

one slope from two

For h(x) = sin(x²) the inside is u = x², the outside is sin u. The red point moves along all three graphs at once.

h′(x)=cos⁡(x2)⋅2xh'(x) = \cos(x^2)\cdot 2x

At x = 1:

inside, u = 1² = 1g′(1) = 2(1) = 2.00
outside, at u = 1f′(1) = cos 1 = 0.540
compositeh′(1) = 0.540 × 2.00 = 1.081

Two mistakes to avoid: f′ must be taken at g(x), not at x; and forgetting the factor 2x gives cos(x²), the slope of the middle graph, not the right one.

Where x² = π/2 the middle graph is flat, so the right one is too: h peaks at x = √(π/2) = 1.253.

3.3 · Implicit differentiation

a slope without solving for y

The circle x² + y² = 25 is not the graph of one function, but it has a tangent at every point. Differentiate both sides with respect to x, treating y as a function of x.

2x+2y dydx=0⇒dydx=−xy2x + 2y\,\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y}

The term y² differentiates to 2y · dy/dx: the chain rule, with y as the inside function. Then solve for dy/dx.

At (3, 4) the slope is −3/4 = −0.75, and the tangent line is

y−4=−34 (x−3)y - 4 = -\tfrac{3}{4}\,(x - 3)

The radius to that point has slope 4/3: the tangent is perpendicular to it, as geometry says it must be. Where y = 0, at (±5, 0), the formula divides by zero: the tangents there are vertical.

3.4 · A curve no formula can solve

the folium of Descartes

The curve x³ + y³ = 6xy loops back on itself. Solving for y is hopeless; differentiating implicitly is routine.

3x2+3y2 y′=6y+6x y′3x^2 + 3y^2\,y' = 6y + 6x\,y'

The product rule on 6xy gives two terms. Collect the y′ terms on one side and factor:

y′=2y−x2y2−2xy' = \frac{2y - x^2}{y^2 - 2x}

At (3, 3), on the curve since 27 + 27 = 54: y′ = (6 − 9)/(9 − 6) = −1, so the tangent is y = −x + 6.

The tangent is horizontal where the top is zero and the bottom isn’t: 2y = x², at (2.52, 3.17). It is vertical where the bottom is zero: (3.17, 2.52). At the origin both are zero and the curve crosses itself.

3.5 · Derivatives of inverse functions

rise and run trade places

The graph of f⁻¹ is the graph of f reflected in the line y = x. Reflecting swaps every rise with its run, so slopes turn into their reciprocals.

If f(a) = b, then

(f−1)′(b)=1f′(a)=1f′(f−1(b))\big(f^{-1}\big)'(b) = \frac{1}{f'(a)} = \frac{1}{f'\big(f^{-1}(b)\big)}

For f(x) = x³ + x, find (f⁻¹)′(2) with no formula for f⁻¹:

1. which a has f(a) = 2?a = 1
2. slope of f theref′(1) = 3(1)² + 1 = 4
3. take the reciprocal(f⁻¹)′(2) = 1/4 = 0.25

The trap is 1/f′(2) = 1/13: f′ belongs at 1, the point that reflects onto (2, 1). Where f′(a) = 0, f⁻¹ has a vertical tangent.

3.6 · Inverse trigonometric functions

slopes from a triangle

Let y = arcsin x, so sin y = x with −π/2 ≤ y ≤ π/2. Differentiating implicitly, cos y · dy/dx = 1:

dydx=1cos⁡y=11−x2\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\cos y} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}

The triangle turns cos y back into x: hypotenuse 1, opposite x, adjacent √(1 − x²), positive since |y| ≤ π/2. The same steps give the others:

3.7 · Selecting a procedure

read the outermost operation

Before differentiating, ask what the last operation is when you evaluate the function. That names the rule to use first.

the function isuse
a product uvproduct rule
a quotient u/vquotient rule
a function of a functionchain rule

Often two routes work. For q(x) = x/(x² + 1): the quotient rule, or the product rule on x(x² + 1)−1:

q′(x)=(x2+1)−2x2(x2+1)2q'(x) = \frac{(x^2+1) - 2x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}
q′(x)=1x2+1−2x2(x2+1)2q'(x) = \frac{1}{x^2+1} - \frac{2x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}

Both simplify to (1 − x²)/(x² + 1)², so q′(2) = −3/25 = −0.12. Blue q′ is the red tangent’s slope: zero at ±1, where q turns.

3.8 · Higher-order derivatives

the derivative of the derivative

The derivative f′ has a derivative of its own: f″, the second derivative, or d²y/dx². It measures how fast the slope changes.

For f(x) = sin 2x, each step is the chain rule and brings out another factor 2:

f′=2cos⁡2x,f′′=−4sin⁡2xf' = 2\cos 2x,\quad f'' = -4\sin 2x
f′′′=−8cos⁡2x,f(4)=16sin⁡2xf''' = -8\cos 2x,\quad f^{(4)} = 16\sin 2x

So f″ = −4f, and the pattern repeats every four steps. At x = π/12, where 2x = π/6:

f = sin(π/6) = 0.500f′ = 2 cos(π/6) = 1.732
f″ = −4 sin(π/6) = −2.000f‴ = −8 cos(π/6) = −6.928

On each sheet the tangent’s slope is the height on the next.

3.9 · Second derivatives implicitly

differentiate the slope again

For the circle, dy/dx = −x/y. Differentiate that with the quotient rule, remembering that y depends on x:

d2ydx2=−y−x y′y2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -\frac{y - x\,y'}{y^2}

Substitute y′ = −x/y, multiply top and bottom by y, and use x² + y² = 25:

d2ydx2=−x2+y2y3=−25y3\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -\frac{x^2+y^2}{y^3} = -\frac{25}{y^3}

At (3, 4): d²y/dx² = −25/64 = −0.391. Negative: concave down.

The sign depends only on y: the top half (red) is concave down, the bottom (blue) concave up. The black parabola shares the point’s value, slope and second derivative, so it hugs the circle there; near the sides, where y → 0, y″ grows without bound and it narrows.
