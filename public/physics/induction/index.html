Electromagnetic Induction

Chapter 15 · Induction

changing flux makes an emf

Currents make magnetic fields. Faraday found the reverse: a changing magnetic flux through a loop drives a current around it.

ΦB=∫B⃗⋅dA⃗E=−dΦBdt\Phi_B = \int \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{A}\qquad \mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}

Flux is the field summed over a surface spanning the loop, in webers (T·m²); N turns give E=−NdΦdt\mathcal{E} = -N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}. The emf is ∮E⃗⋅ds⃗\oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{s} of an induced electric field that has no start or end: it is not conservative. Here a magnet comes to a loop and leaves; ε is the negative slope of Φ.

15.1 · Lenz’s law

the current opposes the change

The minus sign: the induced current makes a field Bind that opposes the change in flux, not the flux itself.

caseB at the loopεBind
N pole approaching← growing−5.1 µV→
N pole leaving← shrinking2.0 µV←
S pole approaching→ growing5.1 µV←
S pole leaving→ shrinking−2.0 µV→
  • flux growing: Bind points against B;
  • flux shrinking: Bind points with B;
  • the right-hand rule turns Bind into I.

It is energy conservation: the loop repels an approaching pole and pulls back a leaving one, so making the current takes work. In solid metal the same braking makes eddy currents, which heat it. Area vector to the left: Φ > 0 when B points left.

Click a loop for its own field, dashed. I and dashed arrows show direction only; one grid square = 10 mT.

15.2 · Mutual induction

one current, another’s emf

A changing current in coil 1 changes its field through coil 2. That flux is proportional to I1, so

E2=−N2dΦ2dt=−MdI1dt\mathcal{E}_2 = -N_2\frac{d\Phi_2}{dt} = -M\frac{dI_1}{dt}

The mutual inductance M is geometry: 247 µH for these 100-turn coils. On an iron core the iron carries the flux through both windings, μr ≈ 1000 times stronger: M = 59 mH. As I(t) rises, B(t) grows left and Bind points right.

Click either. one grid square = 4 A in coil 1, 0.5 mA in coil 2, 0.25 T in the iron.

15.4 · Motional emf

a bar moving through a field

A metal bar moves at v through B. Each charge in it moves too, and feels F = qv×B along the bar.

Free charge slides until + piles at one end and − at the other. Their field E pushes back; charge stops moving when

qE=qvB⇒E=EL=BLvqE = qvB\quad\Rightarrow\quad \mathcal{E} = EL = BLv
B, L, v0.4 T, 50 cm, 0.5 m/s
E=vBE = vB0.20 V/m
E=BLv\mathcal{E} = BLv0.10 V

Nothing closes the circuit, so once the charges have separated no current flows. Stop the bar and they spread out again.

15.6 · A bar on rails

motional emf with a circuit

Rails joined by a resistor close the circuit. The bar is given 1 m/s and let go.

Φ=BLx,E=BLv,I=BLvR\Phi = BLx,\quad \mathcal{E} = BLv,\quad I = \frac{BLv}{R}counterclockwise
F=ILB=B2L2vRF = ILB = \frac{B^2L^2v}{R}backward
v=v0e−t/τ,τ=mRB2L2v = v_0e^{-t/\tau},\quad \tau = \frac{mR}{B^2L^2}1.0 s

The brake grows with speed, so the bar slows exponentially and coasts 100 cm. The power it takes, Fv, is exactly I2R: all 100 mJ of kinetic energy ends as heat.

15.7 · Inductance

a coil fights its own change

A coil’s current makes flux through its own turns. Change the current and the flux changes, inducing a back emf that opposes the change.

L=NΦI,EL=−LdIdtL = \frac{N\Phi}{I},\quad \mathcal{E}_L = -L\frac{dI}{dt}henries, H
B=μ0nI⇒L=μ0N2AℓB = \mu_0 nI\quad\Rightarrow\quad L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{\ell}2.37 mH
U=12LI2,u=B22μ0U = \tfrac12 LI^2,\quad u = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}stored

400 turns on 24 cm, radius 3 cm (μ0nI is 3 % high for a coil this short). Ramping 0 → 2 A in 1 s gives εL = −4.74 mV; a steady current, none. Inductors smooth out sudden jumps.

15.8 · RL circuits

growth and decay, τ = L/R

12 V, 4 Ω, 2 H. Around the loop E−IR−LdIdt=0\mathcal{E} - IR - L\frac{dI}{dt} = 0, so

I=ER(1−e−t/τ),τ=LRI = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right),\quad \tau = \frac{L}{R}

At first the inductor is an open switch: I = 0, all 12 V across it. Later it is a plain wire: I=ER=3 AI = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R} = 3\ \text{A}. Take the battery out and it keeps the current going, I0e−t/τ, while 12LI2=9 J\tfrac12 LI^2 = 9\ \text{J} becomes heat. τ = 0.5 s.

charge sloshing through a coil
