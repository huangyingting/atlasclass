Work, Energy and Power

Chapter 4 · Work

a force over a distance

Work is energy moved into or out of an object by a force. A force does work only if the object moves while the force acts.

W=∣F∣ ∣Δx∣cos⁡θW = |F|\,|\Delta x| \cos\theta

θ is the angle between the force and the displacement. Work is in joules: 1 J is 1 N acting through 1 m.

A rope pulls this 4.0 kg box with 20 N at 30° across a frictionless floor. Only F cos θ = 17.3 N points along the motion. F sin θ = 10.0 N pulls across it and does no work. Over 3.12 m the rope does 54.0 J, less than the 62.4 J a level pull would.

Click the box to split the pull. Arrows: 4 cm per newton.

4.1 · Positive and negative work

with the motion or against it

A force along the motion (θ < 90°) does positive work: the object gains kinetic energy. A force against it (θ > 90°) does negative work: the object loses kinetic energy.

A 0.50 kg ball thrown up at 6.0 m/s: rising, gravity is opposite the motion (θ = 180°) and does −9.0 J of work by the top, where the ball stops. Falling (θ = 0°), gravity gives the 9.0 J back. The bars add up: K0 + Wg = K.

Arrows: v 15 cm per m/s, Fg 10 cm/N. Bars: 12 cm/J.

4.2 · Forces that do no work

at right angles to the motion

A force at 90° to the displacement does no work, because cos 90° = 0. It can change the direction of motion, but never the speed.

A 0.50 kg ball on a 1.0 m string circles a smooth table at 3.0 m/s. The string pulls it toward the centre with T = 4.5 N, always across its velocity. Its kinetic energy stays 2.25 J.

A satellite in a circular orbit is the same: gravity points at the planet, across the velocity, so gravity does no work on it. So does the normal force on a level floor.

Click the ball for earlier moments. v: 25 cm per m/s; T: 15 cm/N.

4.3 · Kinetic energy

the work–energy theorem

Kinetic energy is energy of motion, K=12mv2K = \tfrac12 mv^2. It is a scalar, in joules, and never negative.

The work–energy theorem: net work equals the change in kinetic energy.

Wnet=ΔK=12mvf2−12mv02W_\text{net} = \Delta K = \tfrac12 mv_f^2 - \tfrac12 mv_0^2

The road pushes this 1200 kg car forward with 3.0 kN (drag ignored). After 7.20 m it has done 21.60 kJ of work, and the car moves at 6.0 m/s: 12×1200×6.02=21.60 kJ\tfrac12 \times 1200 \times 6.0^2 = 21.60\ \text{kJ}. The two bars grow together.

Click the car: weight and normal force do no work. Arrows: 25 cm per kN. Bars: 7.5 cm per kJ.

4.4 · Gravitational potential energy

energy of height

Potential energy is stored energy that depends on position. Near Earth’s surface

Ug=mgΔyU_g = mg\Delta y

with Δy measured from wherever you choose Ug = 0. It equals the least work needed to lift the object there against gravity.

This 0.50 kg ball is dropped from 3.0 m: Ug = 14.7 J, K = 0. As it falls Ug turns into K; just before landing K = 14.7 J, so v = 7.67 m/s. Dropped from a height, Ug0 = Kf.

Click the ball to move the zero. Bars: 10 cm per joule.

4.5 · Spring potential energy

Hooke’s law

A spring pushes back toward its natural length, harder the further it is squashed or stretched.

Fs=−kΔxF_s = -k\Delta xopposite Δx; k in N/m
Us=12kΔx2U_s = \tfrac12 k\Delta x^2energy stored

With k = 50 N/m, the 2.0 kg block (K = 4.0 J) stops at Δx = 0.40 m: all 4.0 J is stored, then given back. Work is the area under a force–displacement graph; under kx it is a triangle, 12⋅Δx⋅kΔx\tfrac12 \cdot \Delta x \cdot k\Delta x.

Frictionless. Arrows: 4 cm/N. Bars: 30 cm/J.

4.6 · AP Physics C · Work by a changing force

the area under F(x)

When the force changes along the way, cut the path into thin strips, add FΔx for each, and let the strips shrink. The sum becomes the area under the F(x) graph:

W=lim⁡∑F(xi) Δx=∫F(x) dxW = \lim \sum F(x_i)\,\Delta x = \int F(x)\,dx

A 5.0 kg crate on a smooth floor gets F=F0(1−xL)(1+2xL)F = F_0\left(1 - \frac{x}{L}\right)\left(1 + \frac{2x}{L}\right), 16 N fading to 0 at L = 3.0 m. Integrating, W=56F0L=40.0 JW = \tfrac56 F_0 L = 40.0\ \text{J}, all of it kinetic energy.

6 strips (dashed) 40.22 J; 64 strips 40.002 J. 5 cm/N.

4.7 · Conservation of energy

mechanical energy stays constant

Mechanical energy, E = K + Ug + Us, stays the same when there is no friction or drag.

Ug0+Us0+K0=Ugf+Usf+KfU_{g0} + U_{s0} + K_0 = U_{gf} + U_{sf} + K_f

A 1.0 kg bob on a 2.0 m string, let go at 40°. At the ends it is all Ug, 4.59 J. At the bottom it is all K, at 3.03 m/s. The string pulls across the motion and does no work. Tip: find the zero terms first (v = 0 at the ends, Δy = 0 at the bottom).

Click the bob to split its weight. Arrows: 8 cm/N. Bars: 30 cm/J.

4.8 · AP Physics C · Force from potential energy

minus the slope of U

Since dW=F dx=−dUdW = F\,dx = -dU, a conservative force is minus the slope of U(x): it points downhill, and is zero where U is flat.

Fx=−dUdxF_x = -\frac{dU}{dx}

A 0.20 kg bead on a smooth wire, x measured along it: U = mgh(x).

Click the bead for the tangent. 30 cm/N.

4.9 · AP Physics C · Energy diagrams

turning points and equilibrium

Draw a line at the total energy E: the gap above U(x) is K. The particle turns back at the turning points, where U = E.

stableU minimum, U′′>0U'' > 0: F pushes back
unstableU maximum, U′′<0U'' < 0: F pushes away (hump)
neutralU flat: no force (wire’s right end)

A light atom bound to a heavy one, at E = −0.6 eV. Near the bottom a well acts as a spring, k = U″:

ω2=U′′(x0)m → T=11.3 fs\omega^2 = \frac{U''(x_0)}{m} \;\rightarrow\; T = 11.3\ \text{fs}

Drawn 0.1 nm = 1 m, F 8 cm/nN, 2.6×1014× slower.

4.10 · When it isn’t conserved

friction turns energy into heat

Friction and drag are resistive forces. They point against the motion, so their work is always negative. The mechanical energy they remove becomes thermal energy.

Emech,i=Emech,f+∣Wresistive∣E_\text{mech,i} = E_\text{mech,f} + |W_\text{resistive}|

The 2.0 kg block slides onto a rough floor at 4.0 m/s. With μk = 0.40, friction is f = μkmg = 7.84 N. It stops after 2.04 m: |Wf| = f·d = 16.0 J, all of its K. Emech falls to zero; the total, heat included, never changes.

Click the block. Arrows: 5 cm/N. Bars: 8 cm/J.

4.11 · Power

how fast work is done

Power is the rate of doing work, or of turning energy from one form into another. It is measured in watts: 1 W = 1 J/s, and 1 horsepower = 746 W.

P=Wt=FΔxt=FvP = \frac{W}{t} = \frac{F\Delta x}{t} = Fv

Both 10 kg crates rise 2.0 m at steady speed, so each rope pulls with T = mg = 98 N and does 196 J. The fast one takes 2.0 s: 98 W. The slow one takes 4.0 s: 49 W. Same work, twice the power. Given speeds and times, P=ΔEtP = \frac{\Delta E}{t} is often quickest.

Brief start and stop ignored. Arrows: 0.8 cm per newton.
