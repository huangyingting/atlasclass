Capacitors and Conductors

Chapter 12 · Capacitors

charge held apart

A capacitor is two conductors held apart. A battery moves charge from one to the other, leaving +Q and −Q with a field between them.

Double the voltage and the charge doubles. Their ratio, the capacitance, stays put:

C=QΔVC = \frac{Q}{\Delta V}

In farads (1 F = 1 C/V), never negative. It depends only on the geometry and the material in the gap. These plates are 20 cm square, 5 cm apart: C = 7.1 pF.

One field line per ± pair. Click the plates. Drawn to scale.

12.4 · Stored energy

the work of charging

Each bit dq is carried from − to + against the field already there, through ΔV=q/C\Delta V = q/C. The first bits are free; the last cost the full 100 V.

U=∫0QqC dq=Q22CU = \int_0^Q \frac{q}{C}\,dq = \frac{Q^2}{2C}

= ½QΔV = ½CΔV2: the triangle under the line, 35.4 nJ here. The energy lives in the field, at an energy density (J/m³)

u=12ε0E2u = \tfrac12\varepsilon_0 E^2

12.6 · Dielectrics

an insulator in the gap

An insulator in the gap polarizes: each molecular dipole feels τ = p×E = pE sin θ and turns toward U = −p·E, its lowest energy.

The lined-up ends leave bound charge on the faces; its field Eind opposes the plates’:

E=E0−Eind=E0κE = E_0 - E_\text{ind} = \frac{E_0}{\kappa}

With Q fixed, ΔV falls by κ and C=κε0A/dC = \kappa\varepsilon_0A/d rises by κ ≥ 1.

12.8 · Conductors

in electrostatic equilibrium

A neutral metal sphere in a 1000 V/m field. Its free electrons drift until their own field cancels the outside one within. Then:

  • E = 0 everywhere inside the metal;
  • any charge sits on the surface;
  • just outside, E ⊥ surface and E=σ/ε0E = \sigma/\varepsilon_0;
  • the whole conductor is one potential.

Here σ = 3ε0E0 cos θ, summing to zero; at the poles E = 3E0. A metal slab of thickness t in a capacitor leaves two gaps in series: C=ε0A/(d−t)C = \varepsilon_0A/(d - t).
