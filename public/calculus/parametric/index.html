Chapter 9 · Parametric, polar and vector

curves traced in time

Until now a curve was a graph, y as a function of x. In this chapter a point moves, and its x and y are each a function of time.

This whole unit is BC only: AP Calculus BC Unit 9.

The figure lifts each point of the path x = t², y = t³ − 3t off the paper by its time t. The path on the paper is the shadow of a curve in space, and the height that the shadow forgets is the timing.

Three languages run through the chapter: parametric equations (x and y in terms of t), vectors (position, velocity and acceleration as arrows) and polar coordinates (a distance r at an angle θ). The questions are the old ones (slope, concavity, length, area), asked in the new languages.

9.1 · Parametric equations

x and y, each run by t

A pair of equations x = f(t), y = g(t) gives a point for every value of the parameter t. As t runs, the point traces a curve, in a direction. Here −2 ≤ t ≤ 2:

x=t2,y=t3−3tx = t^2,\quad y = t^3 - 3t

The point starts at (4, −2), rises to (1, 2), passes the origin at t = 0, dips to (1, −2) and climbs back to (4, 2). It visits (3, 0) twice, at t = −√3 and t = √3: a parametric curve can cross itself, which no graph of a function can.

Eliminating the parameter: y = t(t² − 3), so y² = t²(t² − 3)² = x(x − 3)². That equation has the same points but has lost the direction and the timing; the cones on the paper keep the direction, and the lifted curve keeps the timing.

9.2 · The slope of a parametric curve

dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt)

In a short time dt the point moves dx across and dy up. The slope is their ratio, and the dt cancels:

dydx=dy/dtdx/dt,dxdt≠0\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}, \qquad \frac{dx}{dt} \ne 0

For the loop, dx/dt = 2t and dy/dt = 3t² − 3. At t = 2 the point is (4, 2), with dx/dt = 4 and dy/dt = 9:

dydx∣t=2=94=2.25\left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{t=2} = \frac{9}{4} = 2.25

Tangent line: y − 2 = 2.25(x − 4). The blue arrows are the run dx/dt and the rise dy/dt per unit of time.

Horizontal tangent: dy/dt = 0 with dx/dt ≠ 0, at t = ±1, the points (1, −2) and (1, 2). Vertical tangent: dx/dt = 0 with dy/dt ≠ 0, at t = 0, the origin. At (3, 0) there are two tangents, slopes ±√3 ≈ ±1.73, one for each pass.

9.3 · The second derivative

differentiate the slope, then divide by dx/dt again

d²y/dx² is how fast the slope changes per unit of x. Differentiate the slope in t, then divide by dx/dt once more:

d2ydx2=ddt ⁣(dydx)dx/dt\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)}{dx/dt}

For the loop, dydx=3t2−32t=32t−32t−1\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{3t^2 - 3}{2t} = \tfrac32 t - \tfrac32 t^{-1}, with t-derivative 32+32t2\tfrac32 + \tfrac{3}{2t^2}; divide by 2t:

d2ydx2=3(t2+1)4t3\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{3(t^2+1)}{4t^3}

At t = 2 it is 15/32 ≈ 0.469, positive: concave up. Its sign is that of t: the red half (t > 0) bends up, the blue half (t < 0) bends down.

The trap: (d²y/dt²)/(d²x/dt²) = 6t/2 = 6 at t = 2. That is not d²y/dx².

9.5 · Vector-valued functions

position, velocity, acceleration

Write the moving point as one object, its position vector r(t) = ⟨x(t), y(t)⟩. Its derivatives are taken component by component:

r(t)=⟨t2, t3−3t⟩v(t)=r′(t)=⟨2t, 3t2−3⟩a(t)=v′(t)=⟨2, 6t⟩\begin{aligned} \mathbf r(t) &= \langle t^2,\ t^3 - 3t\rangle \\ \mathbf v(t) = \mathbf r'(t) &= \langle 2t,\ 3t^2 - 3\rangle \\ \mathbf a(t) = \mathbf v'(t) &= \langle 2,\ 6t\rangle \end{aligned}

At t = 2: r = ⟨4, 2⟩, v = ⟨4, 9⟩, a = ⟨2, 12⟩. The speed is the length of the velocity:

∣v(2)∣=42+92=97≈9.849|\mathbf v(2)| = \sqrt{4^2 + 9^2} = \sqrt{97} \approx 9.849

The velocity is tangent to the path and points the way the particle is going; its slope 9/4 is the dy/dx of 9.2. The acceleration points into the bend, toward the concave side; any part of it along v changes the speed.

9.6 · Integrating vector functions

from velocity back to position

A vector function is integrated component by component. Going from velocity back to position also needs a starting point, which plays the part of +C:

r(b)=r(a)+∫abv(t) dt\mathbf r(b) = \mathbf r(a) + \int_a^b \mathbf v(t)\,dt

A point on the rim of a rolling wheel has v(t) = ⟨1 − cos t, sin t⟩ and starts at r(0) = ⟨0, 0⟩. Where is it at t = π?

x(π)=0+∫0π(1−cos⁡t) dt=πy(π)=0+∫0πsin⁡t dt=2\begin{aligned} x(\pi) &= 0 + \int_0^\pi (1 - \cos t)\,dt = \pi \\ y(\pi) &= 0 + \int_0^\pi \sin t\,dt = 2 \end{aligned}

So r(π) = ⟨π, 2⟩, and for any t, r(t) = ⟨t − sin t, 1 − cos t⟩.

The figure does the same integral as a sum: 12 short steps v(tk)Δt laid tip to tail. After 6 steps the sum is ⟨3.142, 2.023⟩ against the exact ⟨π, 2⟩; after 12 it lands on ⟨2π, 0⟩. Another start would shift the whole path: the integral gives only the displacement.

9.7 · Motion in the plane

speed, distance and displacement

The rim point of 9.6 is a particle in motion: a wheel of radius 1 rolls right at one radian per second, and the point traces a cycloid.

∣v∣=(1−cos⁡t)2+sin⁡2t=2−2cos⁡t=2∣sin⁡t2∣\begin{aligned} |\mathbf v| &= \sqrt{(1 - \cos t)^2 + \sin^2 t} \\ &= \sqrt{2 - 2\cos t} = 2\left|\sin\tfrac t2\right| \end{aligned}

The speed is 0 at t = 0 and 2π, where the point touches the ground: it stops for an instant, then lifts straight up. It peaks at 2 at the top, t = π, twice the speed of the hub. It moves up while dy/dt = sin t > 0, and never left: dx/dt = 1 − cos t ≥ 0.

9.8 · Polar coordinates

a distance at an angle

A point can be named by its distance r from the pole (the origin) and the angle θ its ray makes with the positive x-axis.

x=rcos⁡θ,y=rsin⁡θr2=x2+y2,tan⁡θ=yx\begin{gathered} x = r\cos\theta,\quad y = r\sin\theta \\ r^2 = x^2 + y^2,\quad \tan\theta = \tfrac yx \end{gathered}

A polar curve gives r as a function of θ. The cardioid r = 2 + 2 cos θ:

A negative r means stepping backward along the ray, into the opposite quadrant: (−2, π/3) is the point (−1, −√3). This cardioid never has one.

9.9 · Slopes of polar curves

θ is the parameter

A polar curve is a parametric curve in θ: x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ. So dy/dx = (dy/dθ)/(dx/dθ), each by the product rule:

dydx=drdθsin⁡θ+rcos⁡θdrdθcos⁡θ−rsin⁡θ\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\dfrac{dr}{d\theta}\sin\theta + r\cos\theta}{\dfrac{dr}{d\theta}\cos\theta - r\sin\theta}

At θ = π/2 on the cardioid, r = 2 and dr/dθ = −2 sin θ = −2:

dydx=(−2)(1)+(2)(0)(−2)(0)−(2)(1)=1\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{(-2)(1) + (2)(0)}{(-2)(0) - (2)(1)} = 1

dr/dθ is not the slope. Its sign says whether the point is moving away from the pole (dr/dθ > 0) or toward it (< 0): the blue arrow on the ray. At θ = π/2 it is closing in at 2 units per radian.

A horizontal tangent needs dy/dθ = 0. At θ = π/3, r = 3 and dr/dθ = −√3, so dy/dθ = −√3·(√3/2) + 3·(1/2) = 0: the top of the cardioid, (1.5, 2.598).

9.10 · Area in polar

wedges, not rectangles

Polar area is cut into thin wedges from the pole. A wedge of radius r and angle Δθ is a sector of a circle, of area ½r²Δθ. Add them up:

A=12∫αβr2 dθA = \frac12\int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,d\theta

The cardioid’s first-quadrant region, 0 ≤ θ ≤ π/2, using (2 + 2 cos θ)² = 6 + 8 cos θ + 2 cos 2θ:

A=12∫0π/2(2+2cos⁡θ)2 dθ=12[6θ+8sin⁡θ+sin⁡2θ]0π/2=3π2+4≈8.712\begin{aligned} A &= \tfrac12\int_0^{\pi/2}(2 + 2\cos\theta)^2\,d\theta \\ &= \tfrac12\Big[6\theta + 8\sin\theta + \sin 2\theta\Big]_0^{\pi/2} \\ &= \tfrac{3\pi}{2} + 4 \approx 8.712 \end{aligned}

9.11 · Area between polar curves

outer squared minus inner squared

Between two polar curves each wedge is a sector of the outer curve with a sector of the inner curve taken out:

A=12∫αβ(R2−r2)dθA = \frac12\int_\alpha^\beta \left(R^2 - r^2\right)d\theta

Take the region inside the cardioid R = 2 + 2 cos θ and outside the circle r = 3. They meet where 2 + 2 cos θ = 3, so cos θ = ½ and θ = ±π/3:

A=12∫−π/3π/3[(2+2cos⁡θ)2−9]dθ=12[−3θ+8sin⁡θ+sin⁡2θ]−π/3π/3=93−2π2≈4.653\begin{aligned} A &= \tfrac12\int_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3}\left[(2 + 2\cos\theta)^2 - 9\right]d\theta \\ &= \tfrac12\Big[-3\theta + 8\sin\theta + \sin 2\theta\Big]_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3} \\ &= \tfrac{9\sqrt3 - 2\pi}{2} \approx 4.653 \end{aligned}

Square first, then subtract. ½∫(R − r)² dθ gives 0.544, far too small: a wedge’s area grows with the square of its radius. Every time: sketch, solve for where the curves meet, decide which is outer, integrate.
