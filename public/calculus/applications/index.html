Chapter 8 · Applications of integration

adding up slices

A definite integral adds up thin slices. Change what a slice is, and the same integral measures an average, a net change, an area, a volume or a length.

The figure turns the region between y=xy = \sqrt{x} and y=x/2y = x/2 about the line y = −1. Every slice of the solid is a washer, a disc with a hole, of thickness dxdx, with outer radius R=x+1R = \sqrt{x} + 1 and inner r=x2+1r = \tfrac{x}{2} + 1. Add them all:

V=π∫04(R2−r2) dx=16π3V = \pi\int_0^4 (R^2 - r^2)\,dx = \frac{16\pi}{3}

Every page of this chapter runs the same three steps:

  • draw one slice, and write its size in terms of x (or y)
  • find where the slices start and stop
  • integrate the size of a slice over that range

8.1 · Average value

the height with the same area

The average value of f on [a, b] is the height of the rectangle on [a, b] with the same area as the region under f:

favg=1b−a∫abf(x) dxf_\text{avg} = \frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx

For f(x)=x22+1f(x) = \tfrac{x^2}{2} + 1 on [0, 3]: ∫03f dx=[x36+x]03=7.5\int_0^3 f\,dx = \left[\tfrac{x^3}{6} + x\right]_0^3 = 7.5, so favg=7.5/3=2.5f_\text{avg} = 7.5/3 = 2.5.

Why: sample f at the middle of n equal pieces and average. The mean is a Riemann sum divided by b − a, so it tends to the integral formula:

8.2 · Motion along a line

displacement and total distance

Integrate velocity to get the change in position. Integrate speed, |v|, to get the distance travelled.

x(t)=x(0)+∫0tv(u) dudistance=∫ab∣v(t)∣ dt\begin{aligned}x(t) &= x(0) + \int_0^t v(u)\,du \\ \text{distance} &= \int_a^b |v(t)|\,dt\end{aligned}

A particle has v(t)=t2−4t+3v(t) = t^2 - 4t + 3 m/s on 0 ≤ t ≤ 4, starting at x = 0. It is zero at t = 1 and t = 3, where the particle turns round. Split there:

interval∫v dt\int v\,dtmoving
0 → 1+1.333 mforward
1 → 3−1.333 mbackward
3 → 4+1.333 mforward

Displacement ∫04v dt=1.333\int_0^4 v\,dt = 1.333 m: the backward stretch cancels one forward one. Distance ∫04∣v∣ dt=4\int_0^4 |v|\,dt = 4 m: every stretch counts. The particle ends at x = 1.333.

The same holds one level down: v(t)=v(0)+∫0ta(u) duv(t) = v(0) + \int_0^t a(u)\,du.

8.3 · Accumulation in context

rate in, rate out

An amount that starts at a known value and changes at a known rate: start plus the integral of the rate.

A tank holds 30 L at t = 0. Water flows in at R(t)=5+3sin⁡(πt/4)R(t) = 5 + 3\sin(\pi t/4) and drains at D(t)=1+tD(t) = 1 + t, both in litres per minute, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 6.

A(t)=30+∫0t(R(s)−D(s)) dsA(t) = 30 + \int_0^t \big(R(s) - D(s)\big)\,ds

Units check: L/min × min = L. At t = 6: A(6)=30+6+12π≈39.82A(6) = 30 + 6 + \tfrac{12}{\pi} \approx 39.82 L.

When is the tank fullest? A′(t)=R(t)−D(t)A'(t) = R(t) - D(t), which is zero at t = 4 and changes from + to −. Compare with the ends:

8.4 · Area between curves

top minus bottom, in x

Slice the region into vertical strips. Each has height (top − bottom) and width dxdx; add them up.

A=∫ab(top(x)−bottom(x)) dxA = \int_a^b \big(\text{top}(x) - \text{bottom}(x)\big)\,dx

y=4x−x2y = 4x - x^2 and y=x2y = x^2 meet where 4x−x2=x24x - x^2 = x^2: at x = 0 and x = 2. In between, test x = 1: 3 against 1, so 4x−x24x - x^2 is on top.

A=∫02[(4x−x2)−x2] dx=[2x2−23x3]02=83\begin{aligned}A &= \int_0^2 \big[(4x - x^2) - x^2\big]\,dx \\ &= \Big[2x^2 - \tfrac{2}{3}x^3\Big]_0^2 = \frac{8}{3}\end{aligned}

Top minus bottom is never negative, so the area is too, even where the region dips below the x-axis. The limits come from where the curves meet, not from where they cross the axis.

8.5 · Area in y

right minus left

When the curves are given as x in terms of y, slice horizontally: length (right − left), thickness dydy.

A=∫cd(right(y)−left(y)) dyA = \int_c^d \big(\text{right}(y) - \text{left}(y)\big)\,dy

x=y2x = y^2 and x=y+2x = y + 2 meet where y2=y+2y^2 = y + 2: y = −1 and y = 2. The line is on the right.

A=∫−12(y+2−y2) dy=[y22+2y−y33]−12=92\begin{aligned}A &= \int_{-1}^{2} (y + 2 - y^2)\,dy \\ &= \Big[\tfrac{y^2}{2} + 2y - \tfrac{y^3}{3}\Big]_{-1}^{2} = \frac{9}{2}\end{aligned}

In x this takes two integrals, because the bottom edge changes at x = 1 (from −x-\sqrt{x} to the line):

∫012x dx+∫14(x−x+2) dx=43+196=92\begin{aligned}&\int_0^1 2\sqrt{x}\,dx + \int_1^4 \big(\sqrt{x} - x + 2\big)\,dx \\ &= \tfrac{4}{3} + \tfrac{19}{6} = \tfrac{9}{2}\end{aligned}

Pick the variable whose strips always run between the same two curves.

8.6 · More than two crossings

split where the curves cross

If the curves cross inside the interval, top and bottom swap there. Split at every crossing, or integrate |f − g|.

f(x)=x3−3xf(x) = x^3 - 3x and g(x)=xg(x) = x meet where x3−4x=0x^3 - 4x = 0: x = −2, 0, 2. On [−2, 0], f is on top (at x = −1: 2 against −1); on [0, 2], g is.

A=∫−20(x3−4x) dx+∫02(4x−x3) dx=4+4=8\begin{aligned}A &= \int_{-2}^{0} (x^3 - 4x)\,dx \\ &\quad + \int_0^2 (4x - x^3)\,dx \\ &= 4 + 4 = 8\end{aligned}

Without splitting, the two pieces cancel:

∫−22(f−g) dx=0∫−22∣f−g∣ dx=8\begin{aligned}\int_{-2}^{2} \big(f - g\big)\,dx &= 0 \\ \int_{-2}^{2} |f - g|\,dx &= 8\end{aligned}

On a calculator, integrate the absolute value; by hand, find every crossing first.

8.7 · Cross sections: squares and rectangles

slabs standing on a base

A solid whose slices have a known area A(x) has volume V=∫abA(x) dxV = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx: each slab is area × thickness.

The base is the region under y=2−x22y = 2 - \tfrac{x^2}{2}, above the x-axis. Slices perpendicular to the x-axis are squares standing on the base, so each side is the base’s width there, s(x)=2−x22s(x) = 2 - \tfrac{x^2}{2}.

V=∫−22(2−x22)2dx=12815≈8.533\begin{aligned}V &= \int_{-2}^{2} \Big(2 - \frac{x^2}{2}\Big)^2 dx \\ &= \frac{128}{15} \approx 8.533\end{aligned}

For rectangles whose height is half the base, A=s⋅s2A = s \cdot \tfrac{s}{2} and V=6415≈4.267V = \tfrac{64}{15} \approx 4.267.

The side is the distance across the base (top − bottom), never a height above the floor.

8.9 · The disc method

turning a region about an axis

Turn a region about an axis it touches along its whole length. Every slice perpendicular to the axis is a disc of area πR2\pi R^2.

V=π∫abR(x)2 dxV = \pi\int_a^b R(x)^2\,dx

The region under y=xy = \sqrt{x}, 0 ≤ x ≤ 4, turned about the x-axis: the radius at x is the curve’s height, R=xR = \sqrt{x}.

V=π∫04(x)2 dx=π∫04x dx=8π≈25.133\begin{aligned}V &= \pi\int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2\,dx = \pi\int_0^4 x\,dx \\ &= 8\pi \approx 25.133\end{aligned}

About the y-axis, slice horizontally and integrate in y. The region between x=y2x = y^2 and the y-axis, 0 ≤ y ≤ 2, gives discs of radius y2y^2: π∫02y4 dy=32π5≈20.106\pi\int_0^2 y^4\,dy = \tfrac{32\pi}{5} \approx 20.106.

The slices are always perpendicular to the axis, so you integrate along it.

8.10 · Discs about other axes

radius is the distance to the axis

For any axis the radius is the distance from the axis to the curve: ∣f(x)−k∣|f(x) - k| for the line y = k, ∣h−x(y)∣|h - x(y)| for x = h.

Turn the same region (under y=xy = \sqrt{x}, 0 ≤ x ≤ 4) about the vertical line x = 4. The slices are horizontal, so integrate in y from 0 to 2. At height y the region runs from the curve, x=y2x = y^2, to the axis, x = 4:

R(y)=4−y2V=π∫02(4−y2)2 dy=256π15\begin{aligned}R(y) &= 4 - y^2 \\ V &= \pi\int_0^2 (4 - y^2)^2\,dy = \frac{256\pi}{15}\end{aligned}

The region touches the axis along its whole edge (x = 4, 0 ≤ y ≤ 2), so there is no hole: discs, not washers.

Write the radius as (farther) − (nearer) so it comes out positive; squaring hides a sign slip, but not a wrong distance.

8.11 · The washer method

a disc with a hole

When the region doesn’t touch the axis, each slice is a washer: an outer disc minus an inner one.

V=π∫ab(R(x)2−r(x)2) dxV = \pi\int_a^b \big(R(x)^2 - r(x)^2\big)\,dx

The region between y=xy = \sqrt{x} and y=x/2y = x/2 (they meet at x = 0 and 4), about the x-axis. The outer radius is the farther curve, R=xR = \sqrt{x}; the inner is the nearer, r=x/2r = x/2.

V=π∫04(x−x24)dx=π(8−163)=8π3\begin{aligned}V &= \pi\int_0^4 \Big(x - \frac{x^2}{4}\Big)dx \\ &= \pi\Big(8 - \frac{16}{3}\Big) = \frac{8\pi}{3}\end{aligned}

Square each radius, then subtract. π∫(R−r)2 dx\pi\int (R - r)^2\,dx gives 1.676, the volume of a different solid.

8.12 · Washers about other axes

measure every radius from the axis

Measure both radii from the axis, not from the x-axis.

The same region, turned about y = −1. The line is below the region, so add 1 to each height:

R=x−(−1)=x+1r=x2+1\begin{aligned}R &= \sqrt{x} - (-1) = \sqrt{x} + 1 \\ r &= \tfrac{x}{2} + 1\end{aligned}
V=π∫04[R2−r2] dx=π∫04(2x−x24)dx=16π3\begin{aligned}V &= \pi\int_0^4 \big[R^2 - r^2\big]\,dx \\ &= \pi\int_0^4 \Big(2\sqrt{x} - \frac{x^2}{4}\Big)dx = \frac{16\pi}{3}\end{aligned}

Twice the washer volume about the x-axis: the hole and the ring both grew.

About y = 2, above the region, the roles swap. The line y = x/2 is now farther away: R=2−x2R = 2 - \tfrac{x}{2}, r=2−xr = 2 - \sqrt{x}, and V=8π3V = \tfrac{8\pi}{3}.

8.13 · BC · Arc length

and distance travelled

A short piece of a curve is almost straight: dx2+dy2=1+f′(x)2 dx\sqrt{dx^2 + dy^2} = \sqrt{1 + f'(x)^2}\,dx. Add the pieces:

L=∫ab1+(f′(x))2 dxL = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + \big(f'(x)\big)^2}\,dx

For y=23x3/2y = \tfrac23 x^{3/2} on [0, 3], f′(x)=xf'(x) = \sqrt{x}:

L=∫031+x dx=23(1+x)3/2∣03=143≈4.667\begin{aligned}L &= \int_0^3 \sqrt{1 + x}\,dx \\ &= \tfrac23 (1 + x)^{3/2}\Big|_0^3 = \frac{14}{3} \approx 4.667\end{aligned}
