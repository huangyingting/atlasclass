About

Learn it by taking it apart.

Atlas helps students understand their AP courses, and gives teachers something to teach with.

A textbook can only describe a moving thing with still words and still pictures. Most of what makes physics, calculus or chemistry hard is in the motion that gets left out: how a velocity turns as a ball climbs, how a slope shrinks as two points close in, how a molecule bends. In Atlas, every page has a figure you can turn, pause and take apart, and it moves the way the idea does.

Seeing it is how many students get it

Plenty of students learn best by seeing. For them, an equation starts to mean something once they can watch it happen. Atlas is built around that. The practice is too: each question sets the figure to its own numbers, and when an answer is wrong, it is drawn on the figure so you can see where it went, before the working plays out one step at a time.

For studentsRead every chapter free, see each idea move, and practice with AP-style questions that show you where your answer lands.
For teachersAtlas doesn't replace you. Put a figure on the board while you explain, or set a practice set for after class.
For anyoneGoing back over college math and physics, or just curious how something works.
